PhD student Nils Krause has developed a reservoir theory of a highly nonlinear reservoir interaction process:

[Evaporative damping in open systems theory of Bose-Einstein condensates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06220). 

Previously regarded as unimportant (a weak surface term), evaporative damping is shown to be comparable to number damping in regimes of high phase space density. Both processes are weaker than energy damping, which is the dominant reservoir interaction in this regime.
All three reservoir interaction process are important near the phase transition. 

The high nonlinearity of evaporative damping poses challenges for analytical and numerical treatments, and these calculations provide a complete phase space treatment of the process, and some useful analytic bounds and estimates on its physical significance. We also carry out a dimensional reduction to provide an effective treatment in systems tightly confined along one or two spatial dimensions. 

This work completes the first-principles reservoir theory of high-temperature Bose-Einstein condensates.
